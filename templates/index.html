Emotion Labeling

A short data labeling and collection task. We are building a high-quality set of human judgments about how emotion comes across in everyday written speech — the kind of quick, unpolished text people actually post online. Machines are still unreliable at this, and the reason is simple: emotion in text is carried by context, not keywords. Careful human labels are the only dependable ground truth.

Instructions

You will read {{ n }} real tweets, one at a time. For each one, choose the single emotion the author is expressing from the six options below, then press Next. After the last tweet you can leave an optional comment and submit.

How to choose

  • Label the emotion the author is expressing, not how the tweet makes you feel.
  • There is no scored right answer and you are not being tested — pick the closest fit even when none is perfect.
  • Not sure? Choose your best guess and tick I wasn't sure next to it. That flag is useful data, not a penalty.
  • You can go back and change an answer before you submit.

Tips for accurate labels

  • Follow where the feeling points. Aimed at a person with warmth → love; aimed at someone with heat → anger; aimed at the situation → joy or sadness.
  • Check the direction in time. Fear is about something that might happen; sadness is about something that already did.
  • Surprise is about the jolt, not the outcome. Good news can be surprise too.
  • Don't hunt for sarcasm. Take the plainest reading unless the tweet clearly signals otherwise.
  • Ignore the vocabulary, read the feeling. A tweet can express fear without ever using the word "afraid".
  • Go with your first instinct. Re-reading a tweet five times rarely makes it clearer — label it and flag it as unsure.
